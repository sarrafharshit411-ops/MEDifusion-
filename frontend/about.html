Platform Abstract

The Multimodal Imperative in Clinical Decision Support

Clinical diagnostic reasoning rarely relies on a single data stream. A radiologist reading a chest radiograph incorporates clinical history and vital signs; an emergency physician evaluating acute dyspnea relies simultaneously on thoracic imaging, oxygenation saturation levels, and symptom onset timelines.

MediFusion AI investigates the fusion architecture required to combine continuous image representations (512-dim visual embeddings), discrete symptom categorical states (15 binary features), and standardized continuous physiological signals (7 vital measurements) into an integrated decision boundary with attention-weighted gating.

Critical Scientific Disclosure

Dataset Pairing Characteristics

The training corpus utilizes benchmark medical datasets calibrated across distinct clinical domains:

  • 1. Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia) by Paul Mooney — 5,856 labeled chest radiographs (1,583 Normal, 4,273 Pneumonia) across standardized train/val/test splits.
  • 2. Symptom-Based Disease Prediction Dataset by MiltonMacGyver — 4,998 structured records covering 15 binary symptoms mapping to Malaria, Pneumonia, and Typhoid.
  • 3. Brain Tumor MRI Dataset — 7,023 intracranial scans across Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary Adenoma, and Healthy controls.
  • 4. HAM10000 Dermatoscopy Corpus — 10,015 pigmented cutaneous lesions across 7 histopathological diagnostic entities.
  • 5. Diabetic Retinopathy Fundus Scans — 35,126 high-resolution retinal images graded across 5 international clinical disease severity levels.
  • 6. Blood Cell Cytology Microscopy — 12,444 peripheral blood smear images classifying 4 essential leukocyte lineages.
Transparent Data Governance: Benchmark datasets do not share universal patient-level identification numbers across disparate sources. Multimodal fusion in this prototype demonstrates the machine learning fusion architecture (gated attention, cross-modal tokenization, and modality-masking) rather than a single patient-paired cohort.
Theoretical Formulation

Gated Cross-Modal Multi-Head Attention Architecture

Let each modality stream $m \in \{\text{vision}, \text{symptoms}, \text{clinical}\}$ be projected into a shared $d$-dimensional embedding space ($d=128$):

e_vision = Linear(512 → 128)(ResNet18(I))
e_symptom = Linear(15 → 128)(Softprob(S))
e_vitals = Linear(22 → 128)(StandardScaler(V))

Cross-attention tokens are formed by stacking available modalities $[e_1, e_2, \dots, e_k]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}$. When any modality is missing, an attention key-padding mask enforces zero contribution:

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax((Q K^T) / √d_k + Mask) V
Gate_m = σ(Linear(128 → 1)([e_m; Context]))
Output = Classifier(∑ Gate_m · e_m)
Safety Bounds

Clinical Governance & Ethical Guardrails

MediFusion AI is built explicitly as a diagnostic decision-support prototype for research and educational investigation. It is not intended for autonomous primary clinical diagnosis or prescriptive intervention without human oversight.

Every algorithmic prediction must be verified by a board-certified medical specialist. In acute distress emergencies (chest pain, stroke symptoms, acute dyspnea), individuals must immediately contact local emergency medical services.
